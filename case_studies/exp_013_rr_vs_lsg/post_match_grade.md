### What the memo got wrong

- **Entry 25 was identified but not followed through — the best estimate was 58% when the memo's own analysis supported 55-57%.** The Skeptic's stress-tested base rate was ~57% RR. Entry 25 was explicitly noted as applicable, with the evidence-favored direction clearly stated ("55-57% rather than 58-60%"). Three independent signals pointed downward: RR's 0-2 home record, LSG's 2-1 venue H2H, and RR's 1-3 in the last 4 matches. The memo then set the midpoint at 58% — above the stress-tested rate — with the justification that "deep-liquidity market prices ($129k volume) contain information our model may miss. Per Entry 8, when the gap is <3pp... moderate deference to the market is appropriate." This is the pattern Entry 22 warns about: when the base-rate estimate agrees with the market and Entry 25 says the evidence favors the lower end, deferring to the market rather than the evidence is anchoring by another name. In this specific case, the anchoring was harmless (58% produced Brier 0.1764 vs 57% producing 0.1849 — a trivial difference in a single match), but the process flaw is clear: the memo acknowledged a directional signal from its own analysis and then overrode it with market deference. When Entry 25's directional evidence conflicts with Entry 8's market deference on a <3pp gap, Entry 25 should prevail — the whole point of Entry 25 is to prevent midpoint-defaulting when the evidence is directionally clear.

- **The form and table-position adjustments partially double-counted the same information.** The Skeptic's initial construction gave +2.5pp for form (RR 4-4 in last 8, better than LSG's 2-6) and +3.5pp for table position (RR 3rd with 12pts vs LSG 9th with 6pts). But table position IS cumulative form — it's the season-long integral of the same signal that the "last 8" window captures. The Skeptic caught this during stress-testing ("Table position reflects cumulative form... partially redundant") and reduced the table adjustment from +3.5pp to +2pp, pulling the estimate from 60% to 57%. The correction was appropriate, but the initial construction methodology produced a 60% estimate that required a stress-test to reveal the double-counting. The base-rate construction should treat form and standings as overlapping variables from the start, not add them independently and then correct.

- **The squad quality adjustment (+2.5pp batting, +1.5pp bowling) was marginally generous for RR given phase-specific weaknesses.** RR's batting edge was correctly identified as powerplay-concentrated (11.73 RR, IPL's best). But RR's death-overs run-rate was the second-lowest in the IPL (9.78). On a 220+ par ground where death-overs finishing is critical, the +2.5pp batting uplift prices powerplay dominance without fully discounting the death-overs vulnerability. Similarly, the +1.5pp bowling uplift gives RR a slight edge despite Bishnoi's 9.48 economy and Burger's 10.45 — LSG's Prince Yadav (16 wickets, 7.83 economy) and Mohsin Khan (10 wickets, 7.56 economy) are arguably comparable. These are individually defensible adjustments but collectively add ~1pp of pro-RR lean that the phase-specific evidence doesn't fully support.

- **The pipeline's incremental value over the market was negligible in this match.** The memo's 58% RR was within 0.5pp of the market's 58.5% RR. A simple "trust the market" approach would have produced Brier (0.415)^2 = 0.172 — virtually identical to the pipeline's 0.1764. The pipeline's purpose is to identify market mispricings; when it converges with the market, the analytical machinery provides confidence (the price is well-justified) but not edge. This is not necessarily a flaw — sometimes the market IS correctly priced — but after two experiments of directional misses (Exp 11, 12), the pipeline returned to accuracy by essentially agreeing with the market rather than producing an independent contrarian view. The Skeptic's stress-tested 57% was an independent signal that deserved to stand rather than being nudged back toward the market.

### What was missed

- **RR's 0-2 at Jaipur was treated as a general home disadvantage when the pattern was scenario-specific.** Both prior losses followed the same pattern: RR batted first, posted 225+, and lost to chases. The common thread was batting first on a flat pitch — not a general home disadvantage. If RR won the toss and chased (which the evidence heavily suggested as the optimal strategy), the scenario that produced the 0-2 record wouldn't apply. A team that lost twice batting first at a venue is not the same as a team that lost twice at a venue. The memo and Skeptic applied Entry 1 unconditionally to the home advantage without decomposing the 0-2 into its batting-order component. This distinction matters: the 0pp home advantage was reasonable as a pre-toss expectation value (toss unknown, so the batting-first scenario has ~50% probability of occurring), but the conditional framework in "What Would Change This View" could have been more aggressive: if RR chase, the 0-2 record is irrelevant (both losses were batting first), and the home advantage should be restored to +2-3pp for that specific scenario, pushing the "RR win toss and chase" estimate to 60-65% rather than 58-63%.

- **The interaction between RR's extended rest period (18 days) and Entry 14's negative momentum mechanism was not explored.** RR were 1-3 in their last 4 matches — a form trajectory that the Skeptic noted as "concerning." Entry 14 was flagged as "partially applicable" (0-2 home losses, not meeting the 3+ overall threshold). But Entry 14's mechanism is "confidence erosion" — a psychological phenomenon that is temporal. An 18-day break between the last loss and this match may attenuate the momentum mechanism: the team has had time to practice, regroup, and reset psychologically. The memo weighted RR's recent form as a negative (-1.5pp reduction from initial +2.5pp to stress-tested +1pp) without considering that the extended break weakens the temporal connection between past losses and current performance. This is not an argument for pricing rest directly (Entry 7's principle that rest/fatigue is unpredictable still applies), but for recognizing that momentum-based adjustments decay with time elapsed since the last match.

- **The memo did not model the specific matchup between LSG's best bowler (Prince Yadav, 7.83 economy, 16 wickets) and RR's powerplay approach.** The memo cited Entry 12 (do not build detailed matchups on unconfirmed XIs), which was correct. However, Prince Yadav was essentially certain to play (he's LSG's leading wicket-taker and was just called up to India's ODI squad). His left-arm wrist spin against RR's aggressive powerplay openers (Sooryavanshi SR 229.5, Jaiswal SR 149.3) on a flat, non-spinning Jaipur pitch is a specific matchup where the conditions strongly favor the batters over the spinner. The bowling quality assessment gave LSG partial credit for Yadav's overall numbers, but his effectiveness on a pitch where the Cricbuzz pitch report explicitly stated "no live grass" and "flat as a pancake" may be lower than his season average implies. This could have further narrowed LSG's bowling edge and modestly increased RR's batting quality uplift.
