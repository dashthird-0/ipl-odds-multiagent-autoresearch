RR's 0-2 at Jaipur in 2026 not only meets Entry 1's threshold but exceeds it — this is the same team, same venue, same season, 100% loss rate. Furthermore, the LSG-specific venue H2H (2-1 in LSG's favor at Jaipur) provides an ADDITIONAL contradiction. Per Entry 21, opponent-specific H2H should be weighted more heavily than general home record for a specific matchup.
- **Verdict:** Home advantage should be set at 0pp or negative. RR's home advantage at Sawai Mansingh is empirically absent in 2026, and their specific opponent (LSG) has a winning record at this venue. Combined, this argues for 0pp home adjustment.

### 4. Dew and Toss — Correctly Assessed but Requires Precise Framing

- **Narrative says:** Chasing is strongly favored at this venue. The pitch report says "if you win the toss, you'll be chasing."
- **Base rate says:** At Sawai Mansingh, the toss winner won the match 53.2% of the time (n=47), and the preferred choice is to field first. In 2026 specifically, both matches were won by the chasing team. However, today's conditions eliminate dew as a factor (humidity 14-15%, dew point gap 31-37C). Per Entry 9, when same-day weather contradicts the dew narrative AND venue field-first win rate is moderate, cap the toss adjustment at 0pp.
- **Gap:** The toss advantage here is NOT about dew — it's about the pitch being a flat road where batting second allows you to calibrate your chase with full information. In both 2026 Jaipur matches, the chasing team successfully hunted down 225+. This is a structural pitch advantage for chasing, not a dew advantage. Entry 9's cap applies to the DEW component, but the structural pitch component (flat road, calibrated chase) is separate and genuine.
- **Verdict:** Supported with nuance. A 2-4pp toss advantage for the team that fields first is justified by the structural batting conditions, NOT by dew. The toss is the single largest swing factor in this match.

### 6. RR's Powerplay Advantage — Real but Narrow

- **Narrative says:** RR's powerplay run-rate of 11.73 is the IPL's best, driven by Sooryavanshi (404 runs, SR 229.5) and Jaiswal (312 runs, SR 149.3).
- **Base rate says:** Powerplay advantage is real but matters most when batting first (setting targets). When chasing, the powerplay advantage can be deployed strategically. LSG's bowling strength is in their uncapped bowlers (40 wickets, best economy 8.92), and their death bowling is where they leak (Avesh Khan 10.88 economy). The matchup is more complex than "RR dominate the powerplay."
- **Gap:** The powerplay advantage for RR is genuine (+2-3pp in batting quality) but is at least partially offset by LSG having the IPL's best uncapped bowling economy. Additionally, RR's own death-overs run-rate is second-lowest (9.78), suggesting they struggle to finish innings. On a 220+ par-score ground, death-overs competence matters enormously.
- **Verdict:** Partially supported. RR's batting quality advantage is real but concentrated in the powerplay. Their death-overs weakness partially offsets it. Net: +2-3pp for RR batting quality, not more.
