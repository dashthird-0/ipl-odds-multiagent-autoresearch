### What the memo got wrong

- **The H2H adjustment of +1-2pp contradicted the Skeptic's own statistical analysis.** The Skeptic correctly identified the 5-4 GT H2H in 9 matches as "barely above a coin flip and well within noise," calculating a 95% CI of roughly 23% to 88% GT true win probability from the sample. The Skeptic's verdict was: "A 5-4 H2H in 9 matches is noise, not signal. +1-2pp maximum." But "noise, not signal" logically implies 0pp, not +1-2pp. If the sample is too small to distinguish from randomness, any adjustment derived from it is false precision. The +1-2pp was then incorporated into the base-rate construction (GT ~61-63% before Sudharsan and dew adjustments), adding unwarranted lean from data the Skeptic's own analysis diagnosed as uninformative. The inconsistency between the analytical diagnosis ("noise") and the numerical application (+1-2pp) is a process flaw: when the Skeptic's statistical analysis indicates insufficient signal, the adjustment should be 0pp, not a reduced-but-still-positive figure.

- **CSK rotation risk was flagged as an uncertainty but not modeled in the midpoint.** The memo and Source Quality Clerk both identified that no source addressed whether CSK would rest players in their effectively dead final league game. This was listed as one of eight band-widening uncertainties (Uncertainty #6). But rotation risk is asymmetric — it can only widen GT's advantage (CSK resting 2-3 key players), never narrow it (CSK wouldn't rest players to strengthen their lineup). Asymmetric tail risks should shift the midpoint slightly, not just widen the band, because the expected value of an asymmetric distribution differs from its midpoint. Even a modest 20% probability of meaningful rotation (say, resting one of Samson, Noor Ahmad, or Brevis) × 4-5pp impact = ~1pp midpoint shift toward GT. The memo's treatment (band width only) correctly captured the range but missed the directional expected-value effect.

- **The pipeline's incremental value over the market was exactly zero in this match.** The memo's 58.5% GT was identical to the market's 58.5% GT. A simple "trust the market" approach would have produced the same Brier score (0.1722). The pipeline's purpose is to identify mispricings; when it converges perfectly with the market, the analytical machinery provides confidence that the price is justified but no edge. This is now the second consecutive experiment where the pipeline produced near-identical estimates to the market (Exp 13: 58% vs 58.5% market; Exp 15: 58.5% vs 58.5% market). Before that, Exp 14 also showed modest convergence (59.5% KKR vs market's implied ~59-60%). The pipeline is trending toward market-tracking rather than market-challenging. While market agreement is sometimes the correct conclusion — the market IS correctly priced — three consecutive convergences suggest the pipeline may be insufficiently independent, particularly when moderate-liquidity markets ($20,610 here) may not be efficiently priced.

- **The scoring norm claims from bd.mcwsports.com (160-165 average first innings) were incorporated despite the Source Quality Clerk rating it as a "low-tier source."** The pitch report section cited "average first-innings total has dropped to around 160-165" from bd.mcwsports.com. The Source Quality Clerk explicitly flagged this: "bd.mcwsports.com is a low-tier source (Bangladesh betting/sports portal). No methodology for the average. Single source for specific figures." Despite this rating, the memo's pitch section incorporated this figure into its analysis of competitive targets. When the Source Quality Clerk rates a claim's source as low-tier and single-sourced, the memo should either discard the claim or flag it as unverifiable — not treat it as context for the scoring environment.
