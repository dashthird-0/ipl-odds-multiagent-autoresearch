### Decisive Factor

Without detailed match scoring data (only the Polymarket resolution is recorded), the specific match-deciding moments cannot be identified. The structural factors the memo correctly and incorrectly assessed include:

1. **The toss/dew miscalibration was the pipeline's primary analytical liability.** The +3pp toss advantage for GT was the single largest directional adjustment in the base-rate construction. If dew did not materialize — as wttr.in's dew-point data strongly suggested — this advantage should have been +1-1.5pp. At +1.5pp, the base rate would have been ~52% GT. With the synthesizer's 3pp concession, the prediction would have been ~49% GT (~51% RCB) — directionally correct and with a Brier of ~0.2401 (beating the coin flip by 0.01 and narrowing the market gap from 0.0439 to ~0.024). The toss/dew assessment alone accounts for the majority of the pipeline's calibration error.

2. **RCB's batting depth on Dharamsala's short boundaries was likely the decisive match factor.** Five genuine run-scorers (Kohli, Padikkal, Patidar, Tim David, Iyer) on a ground where the ball travels further due to altitude creates a high batting ceiling. GT's batting was more top-3-dependent. If RCB posted a competitive first-innings total (the memo identified 200+ as achievable), GT's chase would have required their top order to fire — and if Bhuvneshwar/Hazlewood made early inroads, the middle-order quality gap would have been exposed. The memo identified this scenario in "What Would Change This View" but did not incorporate it into the base-rate construction.

3. **The market's RCB lean reflected genuine analytical signals, not phantom home advantage.** The pipeline theorized (Entry 28) that the market's 53.5% RCB embedded Chinnaswamy home advantage. But the market may have been pricing: (a) RCB's deeper batting lineup suited to short boundaries; (b) the quality coin-flip with a marginal RCB edge on NRR (table-topping by a meaningful margin); (c) skepticism about whether dew would materialize at Dharamsala's altitude; (d) RCB's Dharamsala familiarity (2-1) vs GT's zero history. None of these require Chinnaswamy data. The pipeline's assumption that the market was wrong for a specific identifiable reason led it to lean further away from the market than the evidence warranted.

4. **The pipeline's largest-ever contrarian gap (7.5pp in opposite direction from market) was a magnitude problem, not a direction problem.** The base-rate construction was defensible in its individual components — the errors were in magnitude (toss +3pp instead of +1.5pp, bowling +1.5pp instead of +0.5-1pp) rather than direction. 5. **The coin-flip reality of the match.** Both teams on 18 points. Neutral venue (Dharamsala, not either team's home). Both teams' first IPL match at this ground in 2026. No significant personnel advantages (Salt out by choice, not forced absence). The match was genuinely close to 50/50, and any lean — whether the pipeline's 51% GT or the market's 53.5% RCB — was a marginal call. The pipeline's error was not in identifying the wrong team (a 51% lean is barely distinguishable from a coin flip) but in the analytical chain that produced a 54% GT base rate that understated the uncertainty inherent in a neutral-venue playoff between evenly-matched teams.
