### What was missed

The rain/washout factor (+4pp) is then added ON TOP of the discounted cricket-merit. But if the concern is that the analytical frame resolves ambiguity toward GT, the rain factor (which also favors GT) should be part of the compound-lean consideration, not exempt from it. Five of five nonzero adjustments favor GT (including rain). Entry 30 was applied only to the cricket-merit subtotal (+4pp → +3pp) and not to the full adjustment sum (+8pp including rain). At 25% discount on the full +8pp, the result would be +6pp → GT 56%, not GT 55%. This is a minor discrepancy but reveals an ambiguity in Entry 30's scope: does it apply to cricket-merit only, or to all directional adjustments including structural/rules-based factors?

- **No mechanism exists to calibrate the washout probability against historical IPL data.** The memo notes "IPL playoff washouts are historically near-zero" but does not quantify this. How many IPL playoff matches have been affected by rain? How many have been washed out? If the historical rate is <2%, the 10% estimate is 5x the base rate and should require stronger evidence. If the historical rate is 5-8%, the 10% is a modest upward adjustment justified by the Orange Alert. Without this empirical anchor, the 10% is an estimate floating in analytical space — directionally correct (the weather was severe) but uncalibrated against the historical class of similar events.

- **The Skeptic did not stress-test RR's deliberate bat-first choice as a potential strategic advantage rather than just a moderating factor for the toss adjustment.** The memo treated RR's bat-first choice as reducing GT's toss advantage (from +3pp to +2pp — "their revealed preference moderates the toss-advantage estimate"). But RR's choice could also be a POSITIVE signal for RR: they have specific intelligence about the pitch (having played on it 2 days ago) that supports their batting-first strategy. If RR's choice reflects superior pitch intelligence — "this pitch deteriorates in the second innings, batting first is better" — then the toss adjustment should not just be moderated but potentially reversed. The memo acknowledged this possibility in passing ("RR's DELIBERATE choice to bat first carries information") but treated the information as only reducing GT's advantage, not as potentially creating an RR advantage. On a venue where the most recent match (Eliminator) was won by the batting-first team, and where the team with fresh experience chose to bat first, a bat-first advantage was a plausible scenario. GT won chasing, validating the structural chase advantage, but the analytical framework should have been more symmetric in exploring what RR's choice signaled.

- **The compound-lean discount and contrarian-vindication streak may create a pro-cyclical calibration trap.** The pattern: Exp 19-21 favored team loses → Entry 30 created (discount) → Entry 24 midpoint shift → Exp 22 favored team wins with dampened prediction. If Exp 23 favored team also wins, the discount will have been counterproductive for 2 matches. If Exp 23 favored team loses, the discount is validated. The risk: after the streak breaks, how quickly should the discount be reduced? The current framework (30% discount triggered by 3 consecutive vindications) has no decay mechanism. The discount should perhaps decay by ~5-10pp per match where the favored team wins, returning to 0% when the favored team wins in 2-3 consecutive experiments. Without a decay mechanism, Entry 30 risks becoming permanently baked in and systematically underweighting the favored team in matches where the quality differential is genuine.
