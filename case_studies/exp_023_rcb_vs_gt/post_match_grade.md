### What the memo got wrong

- **The 3.5pp lean below the market was the memo's primary analytical error -- and the first fairly tested pipeline-vs-market comparison was a loss.** The pipeline estimated 54% RCB; the market had 57.5% RCB. Per Entry 8, the pipeline leaned toward its base rate. The stated reasons for the lean: (a) "the market may overweight the Q1 92-run margin (which occurred at a different venue)" and (b) "embed a slight overpricing of the toss advantage at NMS (where the toss winner historically wins only 47.4% of matches)." RCB won. At 57.5%, Brier would have been 0.1806 vs actual 0.2116 -- the market was 0.031 better. This is the cleanest test case for Entry 8: post-toss, deep liquidity, confirmed XIs, no informational asymmetry. The market was more accurate. The 47.4% toss-winner-match-winner stat -- which the memo used to argue the market overpriced the toss -- is a misleading signal: it measures correlation between winning the toss and winning the match, not the directional advantage of bowling first specifically. The market's 57.5% may have correctly priced the Q1 context, toss, and travel as larger factors than the pipeline's more conservative assessment. The immediate lesson: when the pipeline and market are processing the same information (Entry 28 not triggered), and the gap is <5pp in the same direction, the market's marginal pricing efficiency should receive more deference. Entry 8's lean is most valuable when the pipeline has information the market lacks; when both have the same information, the lean should be attenuated.

- **The compound-lean discount was counterproductive for the second consecutive experiment.** Entry 30 at 20% reduced the subjective sum from +3.5pp to +2.8pp, reducing the overall estimate by ~0.7pp. Without Entry 30, the estimate would have been ~54.5% RCB (Brier 0.2070 vs actual 0.2116). However, the 0.0046 Brier improvement from removing Entry 30 is small -- the discount is decaying as designed and its marginal effect is shrinking. The more significant observation: in two consecutive experiments after the Exp 19-21 contrarian-vindication streak broke, the favored team has won. If the favored team wins in the next experiment, Entry 30 should be retired entirely (the original trigger -- 3 consecutive contrarian vindications -- is no longer active, and the discount has been counterproductive in both post-streak experiments).

### What was missed

- **The pipeline has no mechanism to distinguish between "the market overweights factor X" and "the market correctly weights factor X" when both operate on the same information.** Entry 8 instructs the pipeline to "lean toward the base rate when the gap is explained by concrete analytical findings." But the gap being "explained" doesn't mean the explanation is correct. The memo explained the 3.5pp gap by arguing the market overweighted Q1 and the toss. But the market may have correctly weighted both, and the pipeline may have underweighted them. The pipeline's framework assumes that when a gap can be analytically explained, the base rate is more likely correct. This is true when the pipeline has superior information (Entry 28 scenarios). It is NOT necessarily true when both have the same information -- the market's aggregation of many participants' views may outperform a single analytical framework's assessment. A new distinction is needed: when Entry 28 IS triggered (pipeline has informational advantage), Entry 8's lean is strongly warranted. When Entry 28 is NOT triggered (same information), Entry 8's lean should be attenuated -- perhaps halved. The 3.5pp lean would become ~1.75pp, producing ~55.75% RCB (Brier 0.1806 + adjustment ~0.1928) -- closer to the market without fully deferring.

- **Entry 30's scope distinction (objective vs. subjective adjustments) needs formalization.** The Skeptic correctly excluded the toss adjustment from Entry 30's discount because it was "an objective fact." But the boundary is fuzzy. The toss RESULT is objective (RCB won, confirmed). The toss ADVANTAGE MAGNITUDE (+2pp) is a judgment call based on venue data, expert opinion, and historical trends. Excluding the toss entirely from Entry 30's scope because the result is confirmed creates an asymmetry: a +5pp toss adjustment (aggressive magnitude, objective result) would also be excluded, even though the magnitude is subjective. Future Entry 30 applications should distinguish between three categories: (a) fully objective (result known, magnitude fixed -- e.g., a washout rule), (b) partially objective (result known, magnitude estimated -- e.g., toss advantage), and (c) fully subjective (both direction and magnitude estimated -- e.g., quality, travel). Only (c) should be fully discounted; (b) should receive a partial discount (half rate); (a) should be excluded.

- **The Bhuvneshwar-Gill matchup was acknowledged but not analytically leveraged.** The memo noted Bhuvneshwar has dismissed Gill 5 times in 76 balls at SR 105.3 -- one of the most dramatic bowler-batter asymmetries in the stats snapshot. Sportstar confirmed "Bhuvi has dismissed Gill four times, the latest being the Qualifier 1 match." In the Q1, Bhuvneshwar's swing was devastating in cool Dharamsala conditions. At NMS in 33-38C heat, swing is less effective, but the psychological matchup (Gill has been consistently vulnerable to Bhuvneshwar) persists. The memo classified this under "bowling quality = 0pp" because both teams have world-class attacks. But the SPECIFIC matchup (Bhuvneshwar vs GT's captain) is a different instrument from team-level bowling quality -- it targets GT's most important batter. Without detailed match data, we cannot verify whether this matchup was decisive, but the analytical framework should separate "team bowling quality" (Entry 23 differential assessment) from "specific matchup asymmetries" (individual bowler vs specific batter).

- **The pipeline may be systematically underpricing the defending-champion effect in knockout matches.** RCB are the defending IPL champions and have now won two consecutive playoff matches against GT (Q1 by 92 runs, Final). The pipeline correctly applied 0pp for motivation (Entry 7) and focused on squad quality. But "defending champion" is not just a motivation narrative -- it captures institutional knowledge (how to prepare for Finals, experience with high-pressure knockout environments, coaching staff's tournament management). This is distinct from Entry 7's "motivation" framework. If RCB's back-to-back Final wins (2025, 2026) and dominant Q1 performance reflect institutional quality that persists across matches, the pipeline may need a new adjustment category: "knockout experience premium" -- small (+0.5-1pp) for teams with recent Final appearances, separate from motivation. This is speculative and requires more data across seasons.

- **The interaction between Entry 8's lean and Entry 28's informational assessment creates an under-explored regime.** In Exp 22, Entry 28 triggered (stale market) and the pipeline leaned strongly away from the market -- this was correct. In Exp 23, Entry 28 did NOT trigger (current market) but Entry 8 still leaned 3.5pp from the market -- this was incorrect. The pipeline has no rule that modulates Entry 8's lean based on Entry 28's assessment. When Entry 28 says "the market is current," this is positive information about the market's accuracy that should REDUCE the magnitude of Entry 8's lean, not leave it unaffected. The entries interact but are currently applied independently.
