## Challenges

### Challenge 1: Is the toss advantage overweighted at NMS?
- **Narrative says:** Bowling first is advantageous at NMS in 2026 (3/5 chase wins, expert recommendation, Gill himself admits).
- **Base rate says:** NMS overall (2024-2026) is 50/50. The 2026 data is only 5 matches. NMS 2025 was 78% bat-first wins -- the complete opposite.
- **Gap:** The narrative is supported by 2026 data and expert match-day assessment, but the historical instability undermines confidence.
- **Verdict:** Supported but with wide uncertainty. +2pp is defensible given the expert assessment, but could be +1pp (if historical 50/50 dominates) or +3pp (if 2026 trend is the correct signal). Keep at +2pp but acknowledge the uncertainty in band width.

### Challenge 2: Does the Q1 92-run margin justify a quality premium?
- **Narrative says:** RCB dominated GT by 92 runs in Q1 -- this shows a material quality gap.
- **Base rate says:** A single match with an extreme margin. In IPL, large margins can result from one exceptional innings or collapse. GT then won Q2 against RR, showing resilience. Season records are identical (both 60% league win rate).
- **Gap:** The Q1 result is real but is being asked to carry too much weight. A 92-run loss followed by a Q2 win shows GT are capable of bouncing back. IPL teams losing by large margins and winning the next match is common.
- **Verdict:** Overstated if used at more than +1-2pp. The identical league records are the stronger signal; the Q1 result adds a small tilt, not a decisive edge. +1.5pp is at the upper limit of what the evidence supports.

### Challenge 3: Is travel fatigue a real factor or a narrative trap?
- **Narrative says:** GT arrived at 10:30 PM Saturday, had only ~21 hours at venue, less rest and preparation.
- **Base rate says:** No established IPL base rate for travel fatigue effects. Professional T20 cricketers play back-to-back matches regularly. GT played at NMS throughout the season -- they know the venue intimately.
- **Gap:** The travel disruption is factual but its IMPACT is speculative (Source Quality Clerk flagged this). The 1.5pp adjustment may be overweighted for a factor with no empirical baseline.
- **Verdict:** Supported factually but overstated in impact. The disruption is real but GT's NMS familiarity partially offsets it. Recommend +1pp rather than +1.5pp, or keep +1.5pp with acknowledgment that this is the least evidence-backed adjustment.

### Challenge 4: Entry 30 Compound-Lean Check
- Five nonzero adjustments, FOUR favor RCB: quality (+1.5), toss (+2), travel (+1.5), batting (+0.5) = +5.5pp RCB. Only one favors GT: venue (+1pp). Net: +4.5pp RCB.
- Per Entry 30: When 4+ individually justified adjustments ALL favor the same team, apply a 15-25% compound-lean discount.
- Entry 30's decay mechanism: The three-consecutive-contrarian-vindication streak (Exp 19-21) was broken in Exp 22 (GT won as favored team). Post-Exp 22, the discount should decay by 10pp. In Exp 22, discount was 30%. Decay: 30% -> 20%.
- 4 of 5 nonzero adjustments favor RCB (one favors GT). Compound-lean discount: 20% on the RCB-favoring sum.
- RCB-favoring sum: +5.5pp. Discount: 20% = -1.1pp. Adjusted sum: +4.4pp.
- After GT venue offset (-1pp): Net = +3.4pp.
- **Adjusted base rate: 53.4% RCB / 46.6% GT.**

But wait -- Entry 30 was applied to cricket-merit only in Exp 22, not the full directional sum. The toss advantage (+2pp) is not "analytical framing bias" -- it's an objective fact (toss is won, bowling first is chosen, experts endorse). Should the toss be discounted?

### Challenge 5: What would a contrarian GT case look like?
Per Entry 24 (required contrarian synthesis):

**The case for GT at 48-50%:**
1. **NMS is GT's home ground.** Despite the travel disruption, GT know this pitch intimately. 3W-2L in 2026. The NMS support staff prepare practice pitches identical to match pitches. One disrupted night doesn't erase months of home familiarity.
2. **GT's bowling attack has more depth.** Rabada (16 wickets), Siraj (11 wickets, best economy 7.94), Rashid Khan (11 wickets, middle-overs maestro), Holder (7 wickets, 6.86 economy). Four international-class bowlers vs RCB's two elite pacers (Bhuvneshwar, Hazlewood) and Krunal as the spin option.

5. **GT have momentum from Q2.** Won against RR on May 29, showing they can perform under pressure. The "92-run loss" narrative overstates one bad performance; GT are 7W-3L in their last 10 including all playoffs.

**Assessment of contrarian case:** The strongest point is (4) -- Q1 was at a different venue in different conditions. The 92-run margin at Dharamsala may not predict the outcome at NMS. Point (2) -- GT's bowling depth -- is also solid. The contrarian floor is approximately **48% RCB / 52% GT** (essentially a coin flip favoring GT's home advantage).

Per Entry 24's calibration: Since the contrarian case has been vindicated in 4 of 10 applications, and this is a different-venue-from-Q1 scenario with strong evidence, the contrarian case should inform the band bounds but not pull the midpoint below the base rate.
